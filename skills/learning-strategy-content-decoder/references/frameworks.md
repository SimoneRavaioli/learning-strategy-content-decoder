# Reference lenses

These are faithful paraphrases of the user-supplied reference PDFs. The category names and page references identify the original categories. Use the definitions as analytical criteria, not as factual claims about a sample.

## Sources

- **The Impactful Eight** (2026 PDF), pages 1–3.
- **Learning 2.0: A teaching and learning vision for Instructure's strategy**, Melissa Loble, August 2026, pages 1–9.

## Impactful Eight

| Pillar | Pages | Analytical meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Operational efficiency and effectiveness | 1 | Reduce administrative friction, streamline and unify workflows, and free educators and staff for teaching and learning. Modern platforms, cloud infrastructure, and automation can support resilience, sustainability, adaptability, and growth. |
| The science of learning | 1 | Ground design in cognitive psychology, motivation, memory, and research on how humans learn. Include mental health and emotional safety as influences on cognition and long-term memory. |
| Education and industry partnerships | 1–2 | Connect employer needs with educational programs and authentic experiences, including internships, apprenticeships, and credentials co-designed with industry. |
| Assessment lifecycle | 2 | Connect outcomes, curriculum design, measurement, feedback, and program-level insight in a continuous cycle. Support progress monitoring, equity, reflection, instructional decisions, and actionable evidence. |
| Lifelong learning | 2 | Support recurring reskilling, upskilling, and career change through flexible programs, stackable credentials, accessible online pathways, and continued return as goals evolve. |
| Recognition of learning | 2–3 | Link formal and informal experiences, knowledge, practice, credentials, portfolios, and opportunities. Make capability visible for employment, advancement, and further learning. |
| Generative AI | 3 | Use generative AI intentionally in content creation, learner support, educational design, course development, and operations while preserving transparency, ethics, human oversight, sound learning principles, and AI literacy. |
| Evidence-based design | 3 | Use validated research, contextualized results, data analysis, and evaluation to design and continuously improve strategies, programs, tools, and learning environments. |

## Learning 2.0 beliefs

| Belief | Page | Analytical meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Learning is personal | 5 | Adapt learning to individual pace, skill level, interests, goals, needs, accessibility requirements, disabilities, and neurodivergence. |
| Learning needs people | 5 | Use technology to enable meaningful exchange while preserving relationships, judgment, care, educator expertise, peer collaboration, and social capital. |
| Learning earns you trust | 5 | Make progress visible, evidence-backed, and recognized in ways that have real value for learners, parents, and employers. |
| Learning proves itself | 5 | Judge learning by demonstrated skills, competencies, and behaviors rather than exposure or time spent, aligned with the needs of employers and society. |
| Learning never stops | 5 | Make learning continuous, available when needed, and integrated into work and life beyond a diploma. |

## Learning 2.0 strategic gaps

| Gap | Page | Analytical meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Experiential Learning at Scale | 4 | Make learning by doing with real problems and stakes available at scale. Scenario-based, adaptive, personalized experiences can reduce dependence on heavy content investment, physical presence, and institutional effort. |
| Authentic Assessment | 4 | Assess real skills and behaviors rather than easy-to-automate task completion. Use performance evidence and retain educator judgment. |
| Protecting Cognitive Struggle | 4 | Preserve meaningful thinking, low-stakes failure, and effortful practice so AI supports rather than replaces thinking. Make the learner's process visible. |

## Supporting strategic context

- **Pages 2–3:** The vision combines experiential, social, continuous learning toward academic, workforce, and societal outcomes. It develops discipline-specific skills together with judgment, resilience, adaptability, metacognition, and collaboration.
- **Pages 3 and 6:** Use the five beliefs as decision criteria. Their expression varies across K–12, higher education, and corporate learning.
- **Pages 6–7:** Four implementation pillars provide context: Strengthen Our Learning Core; Advance the Full Learner Journey; Establish Platform Value; Empower Every Learner. They are not additional beliefs or Impactful Eight pillars.
- **Page 6:** Canvas, Parchment, Mastery, and agentic experiences may be entry points into a shared foundation. Do not assume one entry point.
- **Page 7:** Investment areas include social and community layers, explicit AI literacy without offloading thinking, and deliberate development and assessment of judgment, resilience, and adaptability.
